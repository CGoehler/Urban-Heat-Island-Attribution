# Urban-Heat-Island-Attribution
Master Thesis "Global Urban Heat Island Intensity During Summer Heatwaves in IFS-FESOM Under Different Climate Forcings" in M.Sc. Earth System Data Science and Remote Sensing at Leipzig University. 

## Abstract
Urban populations are exposed to locally higher temperatures due to the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect. Additionally, anthropogenic climate change will further increase temperatures and cause heatwaves to occur more frequently. While the interaction between UHIs and heatwaves in the present-day climate is well documented, it remains unclear how UHIs respond to different levels of anthropogenic warming during heatwaves. Storyline attribution simulations can be used to determine how observed meteorological situations would evolve under different climate forcings, thereby analyzing the local impact of climate extremes. Here, such storyline simulations of the IFS-FESOM model are used to analyze the relationship between anthropogenic climate change, heatwaves, and the canopy layer Urban Heat Island Intensity (UHII) in 210 cities worldwide. The summer mean UHII is therefore calculated as the urban-rural 2 Meter Air Temperature (T2M) difference for each city, in each of the three different climate scenarios: a counterfactual early-industrial climate (CONT), a factual present-day historical climate (HIST), and a counterfactual +2 K warmer climate (T2K). Under present-day climate conditions (HIST) most cities exhibit positive UHIIs, indicating higher urban temperatures than in their rural surroundings. During summer heatwave days, these UHIIs intensify in most cities, exhibiting distinct patterns across Köppen-Geiger climate classes. The impact of climate change-driven changes in the UHII is relatively small for both past (HIST - CONT) and future (T2K - HIST) climate change. However, the compound effect of heatwaves and anthropogenic climate change exhibits heterogeneous patterns worldwide for different climate forcings. Most cities exhibit an amplification of the climate change-driven changes in the UHII during heatwave days compared to normal summer days, both for past and future climate change. These findings illustrate how storyline attribution simulations can improve the understanding of the complex relationship between local temperature extremes, anthropogenic climate change, and urban-rural temperature contrasts. This can be used to investigate possible future changes in the UHI effect, improving climate adaptation in urban and rural areas in a warming world.

## Data Availability
The IFS-FESOM storyline simulations for HIST, CONT and T2K were downloaded from the European Union’s Destination Earth [DestinE data platform](https://docs.dkrz.de/index.html). All analyses were performed using Python on the Levante HPC system’s JupyterHub at the [German Climate Computing Center](https://docs.dkrz.de/index.html). 

The Jupyter notebooks provided here are intended solely for the purpose of reproducing the analyses conducted for the master's thesis.
